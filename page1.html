Game Development

Building and publishing a playable game in GDevelop, with a written reflection on the process.

Play the game Read reflection

Development process

I started with an early concept documented in my first idea write-up. After beginning work on that version, I realised the scope was too large for the time I had. I scrapped it, restarted from a template, and tried to fold in several new ideas at once. That pulled focus away from the core gameplay and cost more time than it saved.

I then followed a tutorial, adapted it with my own sprites, and built the final version with help from a few online guides. Before publishing, I asked friends to play-test it and give feedback.

Most rewarding

Seeing a finished game that actually works. It met the goals I set: challenging without being unfair, and still fun to replay. I had pictured something closer to a big commercial title, but this project showed me how much time and resources those games require. Even so, I enjoyed playing it myself—the difficulty ramps up gradually, so it does not feel repetitive.

Most challenging

Turning a long list of ideas into one coherent game. I ended up with several prototypes because I kept trying to include features that GDevelop could not support cleanly, or that I did not have time to finish. More than once I made last-minute changes, only to drop the idea and start over.

What I would do differently

I would pick a simpler concept from the start and leave room for updates later. I tend to add features before the base game is solid, which eats into the schedule. I would also track my time more carefully—some sessions ran too long, others too short, and both threw off my planning. Next time I might try a different tool or lean on programming skills to build something more tailored.

Time management and goals

I mostly worked on the game at home, after homework and revision, or on weekends. Occasionally I got too absorbed in it, which cut into study time and led to rushed decisions near the deadline. I did reach my main goal—a published, playable game—but the process would have been smoother with tighter time management. I would have liked to add more content, but finishing what I started was the right call.
